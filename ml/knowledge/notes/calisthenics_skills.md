## How to get your first muscle-up
A muscle-up needs strong, high pull-ups and strong dips. A common target before trying: about 8 to 10 strict pull-ups, chest-to-bar pull-ups, and 10 or more straight-bar or parallel-bar dips. Then practise the transition with slow muscle-up negatives and low-bar or band drills. Avoid wild kipping until the strict strength is there; it is hard on the elbows and shoulders.

## Handstand progression
Build a handstand step by step: prepare the wrists, hold a plank and pike holds, then wall handstands with the chest to the wall until you can hold 30 to 60 seconds with a straight body. Then practise kicking up and balancing away from the wall in short attempts, and learn to bail by turning out (cartwheel) to the side. Little and often works better than long sessions. ASCEND tracks Wall Handstand and Freestanding Handstand.

## Front lever progression
Move through tuck front lever, advanced tuck (flat back), one-leg and straddle front lever to the full lever, holding each for about 10 to 15 seconds over a few sets before moving on. Rows, pull-ups and hollow holds build the strength around it. Train it two or three times a week while fresh. It usually takes many months; that is normal.

## Planche progression
The planche is one of the hardest skills and takes most people years. Start with planche leans and frog stands, then tuck and advanced tuck planche, keeping the arms locked and the shoulders pushed forward. Wrist preparation and patience matter: elbow or wrist pain is a sign to reduce the load. Pseudo planche push-ups build strength alongside.

## L-sit and compression
The L-sit needs straight-arm support strength and the ability to lift the legs (compression). Progress from support holds, to tuck L-sit, to one-leg L-sit, to the full L-sit, on parallettes or dip bars first. Seated pike compressions and leg raises help the hip flexors and abs. Short holds over several sets beat one long struggle.

## Pistol squat progression
Work up through box pistols (sitting down to a bench on one leg), assisted pistols holding a door frame, then the full pistol. Ankle mobility often limits the bottom position: a small heel lift helps at first. Keep the knee in line with the toes and the heel down. ASCEND has each step as its own exercise.

## Human flag
The human flag is an advanced skill. Before trying it, build strong pull-ups, dips, handstand work and side planks. Learn the vertical flag first, then tuck and straddle flags on a solid, fixed pole. Shoulder pain is a reason to step back a level. It takes months for most people.

## One-arm push-up
Work towards it with archer push-ups and incline one-arm push-ups (hand on a bench), lowering the surface over time. Use a wide foot stance and keep the hips square to the floor. Once you can do about 20 strict push-ups, these progressions are a good next step.

## When to start weighted pull-ups and dips
Adding weight makes sense once you can do around 10 to 15 strict reps with good form. Start light (a backpack with books or a few kilograms on a belt) and keep the full range of motion. Weighted work builds strength for skills like the muscle-up and front lever. Log the added load as the set's weight in ASCEND.

## Grip on a slippery bar
Dry hands help: wipe them, use a little chalk, or wrap the bar with tape. Hold the bar with the thumb around it, and train grip with dead hangs. If the bar itself spins or is loose, fix it before training. Calluses are normal; file them down so they do not tear.

## Setting up a pull-up bar at home
The bar should be high enough to hang with straight arms without your feet touching the floor (bend the knees if it is lower). Doorway bars must be fitted to a strong frame and tested with a careful hang first. Never use a bar that moves, creaks or is fixed into weak plaster.

## Balancing push-ups with pulling
The opposite of a push-up is a row: inverted rows under a table or bar, pull-ups and band rows train the back and the rear shoulders. A rough balance of pushing and pulling sets over the week is good for the shoulders. For every push-up session, add some rows or pull-ups.

## Building muscle with push-ups and bodyweight
You can build muscle with bodyweight training if sets are hard enough (close to failure) and you keep making them harder: more reps, slower lowering, pauses, then harder versions such as decline, archer and one-arm push-ups. Eat enough protein and calories. Many people add weighted vests or backpacks later.

## Practising skills (frequency and freshness)
Skills like the handstand are partly balance and coordination, so short, frequent practice when you are fresh works best: for example 10 to 15 minutes before your main workout, several days a week. Strength holds like the front lever and planche are hard on joints, so keep those to two or three times a week with rest days in between.

## Training legs without weights
Use harder single-leg versions: Bulgarian split squats, step-ups, shrimp squats, pistol progressions, Cossack squats, Nordic curl negatives and single-leg glute bridges. Slow lowering and pauses make them harder. Jumping moves add power once the strength is there.

## Dips and shoulder safety
Lower only until the shoulders are just below the elbows, keep the shoulders pulled down away from the ears, and lean slightly forward. Very deep dips or bouncing at the bottom stress the front of the shoulder. Bench dips with the hands behind you bother many shoulders; parallel-bar dips or push-ups are kinder options. Stop at pain at the front of the shoulder.
