## How much sleep you need
The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) advise adults to get at least 7 hours of sleep a night, and teenagers aged 13 to 18 about 8 to 10 hours. People who train hard often feel better near the top of that range. Regular sleep is one of the strongest helps for recovery and muscle gain.

## Sleep habits that help
Go to bed and get up at similar times every day, keep the room dark, quiet and cool, and put screens away for the last half hour. Caffeine in the afternoon or evening can disturb sleep for many hours. A heavy meal or hard training right before bed keeps some people awake. Trouble sleeping for weeks is worth discussing with a doctor.

## Napping
A short nap of about 20 to 30 minutes in the early afternoon can refresh you without making night sleep worse. Long or late naps can make it harder to fall asleep at night. Naps help most when night sleep was short.

## Training late at night
Evening training is fine for most people if it does not disturb their sleep. If you feel wired afterwards, finish hard sessions a couple of hours before bed, keep late sessions lighter, and wind down with slow breathing or stretching.

## Tips for faster recovery after training
The basics do the most: enough sleep, enough protein and total food, water, and rest days. Light movement such as walking, easy cycling or mobility work on rest days can help you feel less stiff. Massage and foam rolling may make you feel better but do not replace sleep and food. There is no quick fix.

## Active recovery days
An active recovery day is light movement on a rest day: a walk, easy cycling or swimming, yoga, or mobility work, at an effort where you could chat easily. It keeps you moving without adding training stress, and in ASCEND a planned rest day never breaks your streak.

## Ice baths and cold water
Cold water after training can reduce soreness for some people, but regular cold baths straight after strength training may slightly blunt muscle gains. Keep it for times when feeling fresh soon matters (for example two matches close together). People with heart conditions should check with a doctor first.

## Soreness that lasts
Normal muscle soreness peaks 1 to 3 days after training and fades within about a week. Soreness that keeps getting worse, very swollen or very painful muscles, or dark (cola-coloured) urine after an extreme session needs urgent medical help. Joint pain or pain on one side is not normal soreness: see a doctor or physiotherapist.
