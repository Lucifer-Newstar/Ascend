## Pain during exercise
Stop the exercise if you feel sharp, stabbing or joint pain, pain on one side only, pain that gets worse with each rep, or numbness and tingling. Muscle burning during a set and next-day soreness are normal; joint pain is not. ASCEND cannot diagnose pain. If it lasts more than a few days, keeps coming back, or came with a fall, swelling or a pop, see a doctor or a physiotherapist.

## Training around a minor injury
With a doctor's or physio's OK, you can usually keep training the parts that do not hurt (for example the upper body with a sore knee) and return gradually: lighter loads, fewer reps, pain-free range only. Do not train through pain to keep a streak; a rest day never breaks your ASCEND streak.

## When to get medical advice before exercising
Talk to a doctor before starting hard exercise if you have a heart, lung, kidney or metabolic condition, chest pain, fainting or dizziness with effort, very high blood pressure, are pregnant, or are recovering from an injury or surgery. Stop immediately and seek help for chest pain, severe breathlessness or feeling faint.

## Exercising safely
Choose activities that suit your current fitness, increase how often and how long you train gradually ("start low and go slow"), use the right shoes and equipment, and keep good form instead of chasing heavier weights. Most people can be active safely.

## Training under 18
Teenagers can and should be active every day, and supervised strength training with good form is safe for them. Under 18, ASCEND does not set calorie deficits and keeps calorie numbers hidden; growing bodies need enough food.
